A task board describes work, but a developer may need to judge whether a meaningful result is ready: a usable import workflow, a first release, or a published field guide. Investigate an optional way to connect purpose, acceptance conditions, supporting tasks, scope decisions, and evidence.

## Investigation

Compare ordinary Markdown pages and links, existing tags or dependencies, saved views, and a small outcome/deliverable convention. Determine which recurring questions actually need additional structure.

Cover deliberately excluded work and changing scope, so later agents can recover why the deliverable has its current shape. A percentage of completed tasks must not imply readiness or evidence of quality.

Keep each repository self-contained and the format human-readable. Avoid introducing a mandatory hierarchy or fixed development process. Check established milestone features in alternatives; assess usefulness rather than assuming novelty.

## Deliverables

- Concrete scenarios where current prose or task organization becomes insufficient.
- Options with adoption and maintenance tradeoffs, including staying with the existing convention.
- A recommended minimal model or a decision to defer.
- Examples of judging readiness and recovering scope decisions from recorded sources.
- Any implementation follow-ups scoped only after the model and need are established.
